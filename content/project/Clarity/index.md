## Overview  
Product leadership wanted to explore a sales email campaign for Clarity, a document-processing product. But the only existing materials were long, dense, and not written for quick marketing use. I was asked to help turn this complex content into concise, persuasive messaging for prospective healthcare customers.

## 🧩 The Problem  
The [Clarity sales sheet](/uploads/ClaritySalesSheet.pdf) and [data sheet](/uploads/ClarityDataSheet.pdf) were informative but **too technical and vague** to function as effective marketing. The Associate Director of Documentation and I were tasked with producing clearer, catchier content that could form the basis of a future email campaign — despite limited marketing context or product familiarity.

## 💡 My Solution  
To ground my writing, I first created a list of targeted questions to better understand Clarity’s value, audience, and positioning. After gathering answers from product SMEs, I wrote two alternative pieces of **concise and benefit-oriented copy**.

Both versions aimed to be:
- Easy to scan  
- Rooted in product functionality  
- Framed around healthcare professionals’ pain points  

### ✍️ Sample 1  
### _Clarity helps me get back to helping patients_  
#### **Skip data entry and let Clarity automatically convert your documents into patient records**

**Less paperwork, more healthcare**  
Clarity reads your incoming faxes, identifies what belongs in your patient records, and pre-fills your EHR — even for handwritten or multi-patient forms.

**The choice is clear**  
- **Eliminate data entry** with AI  
- **Increase efficiency** through learning-based automation  
- **Reduce burnout** from repetitive tasks  
- **Improve patient satisfaction** by speeding up care

## 📈 Results  
As with many cross-functional projects, I wasn’t looped into final decisions or distribution. However, the revised content was shared with both the **Manager of SEO Content** and the **Senior Director of Product Marketing**, and served as a test case for future email content efforts.

This project shows my ability to **repurpose dense technical materials into user-focused, marketing-ready language** — a key skill in product documentation, UX content, and beyond.
